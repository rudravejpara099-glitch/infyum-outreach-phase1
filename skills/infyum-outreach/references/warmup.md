# Step 6: Warm-up and comments

**The goal:** become a familiar face. The prospect should have seen your name around their page a few times, the way a real person who stumbled on their account and liked it would show up. There is no script, no fixed order and no fixed count. **Be a person, not a procedure.**

Before touching anything, look at the profile the way a human would: scroll a little, read the bio, notice what they post, how they talk, what is funny, odd or lovely about them. Then act on your own taste, deciding freshly for each person:

- **Follow:** about half the time (`references/account-safety.md`, Rule E), and when is up to you (first, after a few likes, or later in the visit). Do not follow at the same moment every time.
- **Which posts:** pick from the recent ones (roughly the newest 12) **at random, in any order, and skip many**. Never walk the grid from the top down, and never do "the newest 5". The pinned post is just another post: touch it about half the time. A typical visit touches 1 to 3 posts, with at most 1 comment.
- **What to do on a post:** like only, comment only, both, or nothing. Like more posts than you comment on. Some people only get a like.
- **The story,** if there is one and `story_reactions` is on in `Settings`: sometimes. A like, an emoji or a poll vote, however feels natural; skip it often. **Never reply to a story with text** (a story reply is a message and would bypass the first-message rules).
- **Rhythm:** the number of actions differs from visit to visit and from day to day. No two visits look alike.

## Pace: totally your own judgment

The daily limits, daytime hours, session length and rest days in `references/account-safety.md` always apply. Inside them, decide each pause in the moment, as someone browsing Instagram would. Take the time a real person needs to look at a profile, read a bio, take in a post and think before you act. Sometimes be quick, sometimes drift: stay on a profile and read, open a post and leave without acting, scroll a little. Take your own breaks, the way a person does. Before an action ask yourself "could a script have produced this timing?"; if yes, change it. When a session or day limit is reached, the rest of the work waits for the next session or the next day. After a prospect's warm-up, pause a short human moment before the first message (a few minutes, a different length every time), and keep at least 5 minutes between two first messages.

## Doing it in Chrome

1. Open the profile. Confirm it is the right account and that you are signed in as the outreach account. A login page or an identity check needs the user, and any warning stops the account (hard rule 3).
2. Open a post as the normal post page, `https://www.instagram.com/p/<id>/`, **even for reels** (`/reel/<id>/` opens the full-screen viewer, which has no comment box).
3. **Look at the post itself, not just the caption:** the picture, the reel, the carousel. For a carousel, go through **every slide** before you like it, comment on it or use it. For a reel, watch enough to know what it says.
4. **Like** with the post's own heart (next to the Comment icon, not the tiny hearts on comments). Wait and confirm it turned to "Unlike". If not, try once more; if still not, move on and note it.
5. **Comment:** write the text, **log it first** (hard rule 2), post it, wait, and confirm your comment is visible under your username. If it did not appear, do not post it again; add a `note` line with result `failed`.
6. A story viewer auto-advances to other accounts: confirm the address still contains this prospect's username before every like, reaction or vote.
7. When done: `stage` = Engaged, `warmup_status` = done (`in_progress` if split across the day, `partial` if it ended early), `updated_at`. If something fails halfway, record exactly what was done and **do not replay it**.

Log only what really happened. Each follow, like, comment and story reaction is one Activity Log line.

## Comments

**The goal: get noticed and touch their heart.** A comment is only for catching their eye and making them feel genuinely seen. No pitch, no mention of what we do, no link, no "check your DM". Go deep: react to what really matters to this person in this post (what they went through, what they are proud of, what they are trying to build, what is at stake for them), the way a human who actually feels something would. Never a brainless, obvious or generic reaction ("great post", "so true", "amazing", a bare emoji). It must make sense and be clearly about this post.
- **Not a review, not an analysis.** Do not comment on how the post was made or delivered (the voice, the editing, the structure, the pacing, the hook, the framing, the technique), and never use critic phrases like "doing a lot of work", "the way you frame", "landing the point", "masterclass in". Do not describe or summarise what is happening in the post back to them. Respond as a person who was moved, amused, surprised or reminded of something, about what they said or went through, not about how well they presented it.

- Be a human brain: many personalities, moods, humours, vocabularies and slang. You are never the same person twice (warm, wry, blunt, shy, hyped, thoughtful, cheeky). **Length is anything. Emojis are totally random: none, one, many, no pattern.**
- **Never mimic or copy** any comment, example, phrase or earlier message. No templates, formulas or recurring shapes. Read other comments only to understand the audience.
- It must be clearly about **this post**, in **their language**. No links, hashtags, long dashes, pitching, or questions about their business, prices or how they get clients. A small curious question about the content is fine.
- **The slop check (silent, every time):** throw it away and redo it if it is generic or could sit under any post; sounds like a neat statement, aphorism or built punchline; performs enthusiasm or flattery; follows a tidy rhythm; asks a leading question just to seem engaged; or sounds like something you or anyone has written before.
- Never generic filler ("Love this", "Great post", "Amazing", "Nice"). Never reuse wording across a prospect's posts or across prospects: check the comments already in the Activity Log.
- **Never type a comment keyword.** Many coaches run comment-to-DM bots ("Comment FUNDING"); typing the keyword triggers their bot.
- **Never reply to anyone's comment** (including replies to ours). Comment once and leave.
- Grief, death, illness, elections, conflict or anything you cannot word safely: like only, or one short plain line of warmth. Faith and politics: respectful, no opinions.
- Not done by Claude: resharing their posts on the user's own story.
